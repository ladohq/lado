## 1. Summary

- **A strict proxy inside the runner showed no holes.** As uid 1000 and as root in the
  sandbox it reached none of the forbidden targets:
  - the runner's localhost, the runner's and the host's addresses, the other sandbox S2,
    metadata `169.254.169.254` (with `Metadata: true`);
  - an IP in any notation (decimal, hexadecimal, octal, short, IPv4-mapped);
  - allowed names that point to private addresses (`localtest.me`, `*.nip.io`);
  - a name outside the allowlist: it does not even resolve it, so there is no leak channel
    through DNS;
  - malformed requests, smuggling and long lines it refuses and keeps working.

  The runner's rules did not change after all the attempts. In scheme B (the proxy in its
  own container) the result is the same.
- **An open proxy (no allowlist, passes headers through) is a hole in any layout.** Inside
  the runner it is markedly worse:
  - **it is a bridge between sandboxes** (CONNECT and GET to S2 go through);
  - it reaches the services on the runner's localhost, where the gateway and the channel to
    the panel would live, the host, and Azure's metadata with `Metadata: true`;
  - in scheme B it does not reach S2, but the host, the metadata and its own localhost are
    open to it.
- **Kernel rules on the proxy's uid** (`iptables -m owner` in OUTPUT: no private, loopback
  or link-local addresses) close every network target of the open proxy. Policy questions
  remain: an IP literal, port 80, resolving foreign names. This is a second layer that does
  not depend on the proxy's code.
- **Without `ip6tables` REJECT from `veth+`** the sandbox reaches the runner's services over
  IPv6 link-local (`[fe80::…%eth0]:9997`) with any proxy, as in [E9](m0-e9.md). With these
  rules it is closed.
- **Decision by the threshold: the strict proxy has no hole → the proxy may live inside the
  runner.** The conditions: strict parsing and the address check before connecting,
  `ip6tables`, per-client limits. A proxy that is not strict is full of holes and by the
  threshold goes into a separate container; inside the runner such a proxy is also a bridge
  between sessions.

## 3. What was done

### 3.1 Two proxies (`strict_proxy.py`, `open_proxy.py`)

- **The open one** is what round 2's proxy becomes when finished. It accepts CONNECT to
  anywhere and any method with an absolute URI. It passes the client's headers (`Metadata`,
  `Metadata-Flavor`) on and strips `Proxy-*`.
- **The strict one** is the candidate for LADO:
  - **it accepts only CONNECT**, and answers 405 to anything else;
  - **it parses the request head strictly**:
    - lines only with CRLF, without control bytes and obs-fold;
    - at most 4 KiB per line, 50 headers and 16 KiB in all;
    - 10 s for the head; on a violation 414/431/408;
  - **the target is `name:port`**: a DNS name only, without a trailing dot; IP literals in
    any notation are refused; the port from a list (443);
  - **the allowlist is checked before DNS**: a name outside the list is not resolved;
  - **all of the name's addresses must be global** (not private, not loopback, not
    link-local, not CGNAT, not IPv4-mapped); the connection goes to the address already
    checked, without a second resolve;
  - at most 256 clients at once; the reason for a refusal goes into `X-Refused` and the log.

Both proxies run as uid 65534.

### 3.2 The layouts

- **Inside the runner** (`inrunner.sh`, `inrunner-in.sh`):
  - round 2's R+net runner (`runner.json`, six capabilities, `apparmor=unconfined`, as in
    round 2's isolation phase);
  - sandboxes S1 and S2 under runsc in their own netns;
  - the proxy listens on `0.0.0.0:3128` in the runner;
  - the rules: nothing is forwarded from `veth+` (`FORWARD REJECT`); in `INPUT` from `veth+`
    only the proxy's port and the "gateway" port 7000 are open; `ip6tables` INPUT/FORWARD
    REJECT from `veth+` on (switched off once for a check);
  - the `+own` variant: OUTPUT for uid 65534 refuses `0/8, 127/8, 10/8, 172.16/12,
    192.168/16, 169.254/16, 100.64/10` and `::1, fe80::/10, fc00::/7, ::ffff:0:0/96`; the
    exception is Docker's DNS (all of `127.0.0.11`, details in section 5);
  - decoy services listen in the runner: `127.0.0.1:9999`, `0.0.0.0:9998`, `[::]:9997` and
    the "gateway" `0.0.0.0:7000`; on the host `0.0.0.0:9998` and `[::]:9997`.
- **Scheme B** (`scheme.sh`):
  - E14's scheme: sessions under `--runtime=runsc` on the host, a bridge per session,
    `DOCKER-USER` over the pool, `INPUT` and `ip6tables` REJECT;
  - the proxy in its own container in `e3-egress`;
  - the proxy's container has a service on `127.0.0.1:9999`: this is how a gateway in the
    same container would look.

The probe (`e3probe.py`) runs in S1 as uid 1000 and as root. Each variant starts with new
sandboxes.

## 4. What it means for the plan

Proposals for the supervisor:
- **E3's threshold:** the proxy may stay inside the runner if it is strict. Then a separate
  container for the proxy is not needed. The conditions for M4b:
  - parsing of CONNECT only, strict;
  - the allowlist before DNS;
  - a check of every address and the connection to the checked address;
  - IP literals refused, ports from a list;
  - `ip6tables` REJECT from `veth+`;
  - the proxy under its own uid.

  The tests of table 3.3 should be kept as the proxy's regression suite (M4b's "T3 closed
  by tests").
- **The second layer: owner rules on the proxy's uid** (OUTPUT without private, loopback
  and link-local addresses, except Docker's DNS). They are cheap and turn a bug in the
  proxy's code from "a bridge between sessions and to the gateway" into a failed request. I
  recommend switching them on together with the strict proxy.
- **What must not be done:** leaving a proxy without an allowlist in the runner (for
  example round 2's `proxy.py` in M3's observe mode) without owner rules. Inside the runner
  it is a bridge between sandboxes and a path to the runner's localhost. For M3 ("the proxy
  in observe mode") this means: from day one it runs under its own uid, with owner rules and
  with the address check before connecting, even while there is no allowlist yet.
- **Per-client limits:** one client from S1 took all 256 slots of the strict proxy, and an
  allowed request got 503 meanwhile. A shared proxy is a resource shared by the sessions
  (T6). A connection limit per sandbox (by its veth address) is needed, not one for all.
- **What the Agent API gateway needs** (it will be reachable from the sandbox in any case):
  - its own port, and only that, in `INPUT` from `veth+`: in this layout the sandbox reached
    7000 and none of the runner's other ports;
  - no services on the runner's `0.0.0.0` and `[::]` except the proxy and the gateway;
    everything else only on `127.0.0.1` and behind the proxy's owner rules;
  - strict parsing, as in the proxy: limits on lines and sizes, timeouts, no obs-fold and no
    bare LF;
  - its own check of the agent's token (M1) and per-sandbox limits;
  - answering only by its own sandbox's veth address, so a sandbox cannot speak in a
    neighbour's name.

## 5. Problems and surprises; what was not done

- **The first run (`46002361997`)** gave no data inside the runner: the list of ports in the
  probe's JSON was put together wrong (`9999 22`). Fixed.
- **The second run (`46004690468`) inside the runner** gave wrong "strict" columns. Root in
  the R+net runner cannot kill a process of another uid: the six capabilities do not include
  `CAP_KILL`. The old open proxy stayed on the port, and the strict one did not start. In
  the second run this is seen in the log (`open_proxy.py` in the strict columns). In the
  third the proxy is stopped by its own uid, and its start is checked. For the runner this
  is a useful fact too: the runner's root cannot signal the proxy's and the gateway's
  processes under another uid.
- **Docker's DNS in the owner rules.** In the `+own` variant of the second run the proxy
  could not resolve names. Docker redirects `127.0.0.11:53` to a random port before the
  filter table, so a rule with `--dport 53` does not match. Fixed: the whole address
  `127.0.0.11` is allowed. Docker's built-in DNS is the only exception to the loopback ban.
- Scheme B was taken in the second run (`46004690468`); everything there was right. It was
  not repeated in the third.
- Not done:
  - GCP metadata (`Metadata-Flavor: Google`) on real GCP: the runners are Azure and AWS; the
    header was sent, but there is no GCP target;
  - arm64;
  - the proxy's effect on speed;
  - TLS interception and checking SNI against the CONNECT name (outside E3's scope).
